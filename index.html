05. The squishy bits

What this calculator doesn't capture

Numbers don't tell the whole story. The case for staying or going has dimensions a spreadsheet cannot model.

  • Family, friends, identity. The most important variable is also the hardest to put in a column. Distance from home, family, surf breaks, and the country that shaped you is a real cost. Different people weight it differently. The calculator does not.
  • Housing competition in Brisbane. Vacancy is 0.6% in Brisbane as of March 2026. You will queue at flat viewings alongside thirty other applicants. The bond and Airbnb numbers assume you find something within two to three weeks. If it takes longer, add accordingly.
  • Job market access. The calculator assumes you can land a fresh-MSc role in either country at the salary you set. Australia has roughly ten times more biotech jobs advertised per capita than NZ does in microbiology. Probability of landing matters, and the calculator treats it as 100%.
  • Career trajectory. Year-one salaries are one thing. Five-year salaries are another. The Australian biotech and pharma sector grows ladders for graduates that NZ largely does not. The honest 10-year picture is wider than this calculator shows.
  • Tax residency complexity. If you have NZ investments, dividend income, or rental property while in Australia, double-tax treaty rules apply. Worth talking to an accountant before you leave.
  • Healthcare and ACC. NZ has ACC, which Australia does not. Australian Medicare covers GP visits but waiting times for bulk-billed clinics in Brisbane are real. Budget AU$60–90 per visit if you need private. Not modelled here.
  • The British passport option. For those who hold one (or any EU/UK working right), the UK biotech corridor is a third option that pays similarly to AU, sits in a similar timezone to home, and avoids the trans-Tasman calculus entirely. Worth a separate calculation.
  • Option value. Being in Brisbane gives you an easier pivot to Sydney, Melbourne, London, or the US than being in Christchurch does. That optionality has real value. Hard to quantify but it is positive on the Australia side.
